# How the words are written

The layout can be perfect and the page still reads as generated, because of the
copy. This is what separates them.

## The substitution test

Take any sentence on the page. Swap the product for a different product in the
same trade. If the sentence still works, it says nothing.

> "Premium quality materials, crafted with care." → works for a chair, a serum
> and a drone. Delete it.
> "62 cm handle, so you reach the middle of your own back." → works for exactly
> one product. Keep it.

Every heading and every body paragraph must fail the substitution test.

## What a sentence must carry

At least one of: **a number · a material · a duration · a place · a person · a
named process.** A sentence with none of those is an adjective sentence and
belongs in the bin.

Banned words unless the merchant used them first: premium, high-quality,
innovative, cutting-edge, revolutionary, game-changing, elevate, unleash,
seamless, curated, bespoke, luxurious, perfect for everyone.

## Headings

- The `display` heading states ONE thing. Not the product name, not a feature
  list. "Not a starter kit. This is the complete system." says one thing.
- Section headings say what the section is about, never restate the product
  name. "The math" over a price band. "Everything the salon has" over an
  inventory. Not "Our Products".
- Eyebrows are 1–3 words, uppercase: `THE MATH` · `INSIDE THE BOX` · `SPEC 04`.
- Write the line break yourself. A `display` that wraps in three lines you chose
  reads as typeset; one that wraps wherever reads as pasted.

## Body

- `body-lead` under a heading: 18–22 words, one sentence, no clause pile-up.
- `body` in a card: 2–3 sentences, 28–45 words.
- Never open two consecutive paragraphs with the same word.
- Say the objection out loud, then answer it. "A salon gel manicure runs $60–80.
  The kit is $89." beats "Great value".

## Reviews

- Three to six. **Vary the length** — six of identical length read as invented,
  because they are.
- One card at four stars with a mild reservation. It makes the other five
  believable.
- Each review names the worry, then what actually happened, with one concrete
  detail: a number, a place, a length of time.
- Signature is `— First name L.` No exclamation marks. No "Amazing product!!!"

## Numbers

- Every stat carries its denominator: `92% of 2,000 buyers`, not `92%`.
- Every measured value carries its unit and, where it matters, its condition:
  `30 hours with ANC off`.
- Prices: `compareAt` 25–40% above `price`. `$980` struck above `$119` reads as
  a lie even as a placeholder.
- Never invent a certification, an award, a delivery window, a guarantee or a
  review count that the merchant did not give you. If you need proof and have
  none, write the section around what IS known — the material, the process, the
  return policy — and leave the number out.

## Regulated trades

For supplements, skincare, medical, personal-care devices and anything
health-adjacent: use structure/function language, never disease language.

> "supports muscle relaxation after a long day" ✓
> "cures back pain" ✗ — fails advertising policy and gets the page filtered out
> of AI search results

Include what the product is NOT, and who should not use it. A contraindication
block is a trust signal, not a weakness.

## Language

Write in the language the merchant wrote their brief in. If they wrote in
Vietnamese, the page is in Vietnamese — including the buttons, the eyebrows and
the review signatures. Never mix two languages on one page.
